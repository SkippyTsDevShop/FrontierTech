Tokenized Treasuries Went From Buzzword to a Real Market — Here's the 2026 Number

For years, 'tokenized real-world assets' was a slide in a pitch deck. In 2026, it's a $14.8 billion market with BlackRock, Circle, and Franklin Templeton all competing for share — and it's arguably the clearest proof blockchain's boring use cases are the ones that stuck.

By the FrontierTech Desk·September 21, 2026·7 min read

Illustration: FrontierTech.news

"Real-world asset tokenization" spent years as one of crypto's most-pitched, least-delivered ideas — a slide in every conference deck promising that stocks, bonds, and real estate would soon live on a blockchain. In 2026, at least one corner of that pitch has actually happened: tokenized U.S. Treasury funds are a real, multi-billion-dollar market, with some of the largest asset managers on Earth competing directly for share of it.

The actual number

Tokenized U.S. Treasury funds currently hold a combined $14.82 billion on-chain, spread across 25 distinct products with more than 80,000 holders, according to RWA.xyz's live tracking data. That's real capital sitting in tokenized versions of the most conservative asset class in finance — not a pilot program or a testnet demo, but functioning products people are actually putting money into.

Who's actually running this market

The competition at the top is a mix of crypto-native firms and traditional finance heavyweights, which is itself notable. Circle's USYC leads at $2.51 billion, just ahead of BlackRock's BUIDL (issued via Securitize) at $2.30 billion and Ondo's USDY at $2.25 billion. Franklin Templeton's iBENJI ($1.70 billion) and WisdomTree's WTGXX ($1.23 billion) round out the top five. That BlackRock and Franklin Templeton — two of the largest asset managers in the world — are running tokenized products at meaningful scale alongside crypto-native issuers is a different signal than a startup claiming the same thing would be.

That the largest asset managers in the world are now running tokenized products at meaningful scale, not just crypto-native startups, is a different signal than the same pitch would have sent five years ago.

Why this is the RWA use case that actually stuck

Tokenized Treasuries succeeded where a lot of other tokenization pitches stalled because the underlying case is simple: a Treasury fund is already a fungible, well-understood, low-risk instrument, and putting it on-chain mostly just makes it faster to move, trade, and use as collateral without changing what it fundamentally is. That's a much narrower, more achievable claim than "tokenize real estate" or "tokenize private equity," and it's part of why — as we've written before — broader asset tokenization is the enterprise blockchain use case with the most consistent pilot-to-production conversion. The broader stablecoin infrastructure underneath all of this is doing real volume too: stablecoins processed an estimated $46 trillion in transaction volume in 2025 alone, roughly 20 times PayPal's volume and approaching Visa's, according to a16z crypto's 2026 industry analysis.

Where it's heading next

The next wave being discussed in the industry isn't simple tokenization of existing assets — it's what a16z calls "crypto-native" instruments: perpetual futures on emerging-market equities, tokenized private credit and pre-IPO company shares, and money market fund products, all designed to offer liquidity that a literal on-chain copy of a traditional asset can't. On the institutional side, legacy banks running decades-old mainframe and COBOL infrastructure are adopting stablecoins and tokenized deposits as a way to modernize without ripping out their core systems entirely — a more incremental, less disruptive path into tokenization than the industry originally pitched.

The caveats worth keeping in view

This market isn't a one-way line up and to the right: RWA.xyz's own tracking shows total tokenized Treasury value down about 5.25% over a recent 30-day period, a reminder that flows into and out of these products move with broader crypto and rate conditions, not just adoption trends. Concentration is also real — five issuers control the large majority of the $14.8 billion market, and regulatory treatment of tokenized securities is still evolving in multiple jurisdictions. The honest read is that tokenized Treasuries have crossed from theoretical to real at meaningful scale, not that the broader RWA thesis has been fully proven out yet.
