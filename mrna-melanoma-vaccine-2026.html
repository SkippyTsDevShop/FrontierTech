The First mRNA Cancer Vaccine Just Cleared a Phase 3 Trial

For years, cancer vaccines were a promising idea that kept failing in the largest trials. Merck and Moderna's personalized melanoma vaccine just became the first to succeed in a randomized Phase 3 study — a result oncologists have waited a decade for.

By the FrontierTech Science & Health Desk·September 27, 2026·7 min read

Illustration: FrontierTech.news

Cancer vaccines have a long, discouraging history: a genuinely promising idea that has failed, again and again, the moment it reached a large randomized trial. On August 19, 2026, that streak broke. Merck and Moderna announced that INTerpath-001, a Phase 3 trial of their personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine, met both of its primary endpoints — the first time a neoantigen cancer vaccine has succeeded in a randomized, late-stage study of this kind.

What actually got tested

The vaccine, called intismeran autogene (also known by its earlier names mRNA-4157 and V940), isn't off-the-shelf. Each dose is manufactured individually: a patient's tumor is sequenced, an algorithm predicts which mutated proteins (neoantigens) are most likely to trigger an immune response, and Moderna builds an mRNA vaccine encoding up to 34 of them, specific to that one patient's cancer. INTerpath-001 enrolled 1,137 patients with completely resected (surgically removed) stage IIB-IV melanoma, randomizing them 2:1 to receive the personalized vaccine alongside Merck's Keytruda (pembrolizumab), or Keytruda alone.

The result, and what's still missing

The trial met its primary endpoint, recurrence-free survival (RFS), and its key secondary endpoint, distant metastasis-free survival (DMFS), both improved with a statistically significant, "clinically meaningful" margin over Keytruda alone, according to the companies. What Merck and Moderna have not yet released is the actual numbers — the hazard ratios and percentage improvements that would let an oncologist size up exactly how much benefit this adds. That data is being saved for presentation at a subsequent medical meeting, which is common practice, but it does mean the current headline is real, and still incomplete.

Why the earlier data was already convincing

INTerpath-001 was closely watched because the smaller Phase 2b trial (KEYNOTE-942) that preceded it had already shown a large effect: a 49% reduction in the risk of recurrence or death, and a 59% reduction in the risk of distant metastasis or death, versus Keytruda alone. Those numbers held up at 3-year and even 5-year follow-up, which is unusually durable for an early-phase oncology result. Phase 3's job was simply to confirm that effect held at a much larger scale and wasn't a statistical fluke — and it did.

Why this is bigger than one drug

The significance goes beyond melanoma. Intismeran autogene is built on a general-purpose platform: sequence the tumor, predict the neoantigens, manufacture a personalized vaccine, repeat. Moderna and Merck are already running earlier-stage trials applying the same approach to lung cancer and other solid tumors. It's a different biotech strategy than the gene-editing work we've covered elsewhere, but part of the same broader shift toward medicine built around one patient's specific biology rather than a one-size-fits-all drug. A confirmed Phase 3 win in melanoma is the proof-of-concept the rest of that pipeline needed.

What happens next

Merck and Moderna say they'll present full trial data at an upcoming international medical meeting and begin engaging regulators on a filing for intismeran autogene plus Keytruda in resected melanoma. Overall survival — the gold-standard endpoint regulators and oncologists care about most — is still being tracked as a secondary endpoint and will take longer to read out. Until the full data set and a regulatory decision arrive, this is a confirmed, statistically significant win with the specifics still to come, not yet an approved treatment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is intismeran autogene?

Intismeran autogene (also known as mRNA-4157 or V940) is a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine made individually for each patient: it's manufactured to encode up to 34 neoantigens specific to that patient's own tumor, designed to be given alongside Merck's Keytruda after surgery to remove melanoma.

Did the Phase 3 trial release exact numbers?

Not yet. Merck and Moderna confirmed the trial met its recurrence-free survival and distant metastasis-free survival endpoints with a statistically significant, clinically meaningful benefit, but the specific hazard ratios and percentage improvements are being reserved for presentation at a future medical conference.

How much did the earlier Phase 2b trial show?

The Phase 2b KEYNOTE-942 trial that preceded this Phase 3 study showed a 49% reduction in the risk of recurrence or death and a 59% reduction in the risk of distant metastasis or death, with the benefit still holding up at 3- and 5-year follow-up.

Is this vaccine approved yet?

No. Merck and Moderna say they will engage with regulators on a filing following this Phase 3 result, but as of September 2026 intismeran autogene remains investigational and is not an approved treatment.
